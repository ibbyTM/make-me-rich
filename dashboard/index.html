How it works

Six stages, in order, every time.

Nothing skips ahead. A source that looks perfect on classification still waits in the same queue as one that looks doubtful.

1
Discover

Find candidate agents

A geography and a set of keywords go in. A list of candidate agent websites comes out — found by search, not invented.

2
Classify

Read the structure

Every candidate is fetched and inspected: is the listing data embedded in the page, served by an API, rendered client-side, or locked behind a form no scraper should touch.

3
Review gate

Wait for a person

Nothing is trusted on a confidence score alone. Every source is written to the register as pending until someone confirms it, whatever the classifier thinks.

4
Scrape — read-only

Read, never write

Approved sources are read on a schedule. Nothing is submitted, nothing is logged into, nothing changes on the other end.

5
Stage-0 filter

Rule out for free

Geography, size, price, keywords — checked against your criteria before anything reaches a paid model. Most listings are ruled out here, at no cost.

6
Match

Log the reasoning

What's left is scored against your criteria and kept on file — with the reasoning stored alongside the result, not just the verdict.

The gate

Nothing is trusted by default.

The review gate is the one stage every source passes through regardless of how it was found or how confident the classifier was.

Read-only

No source is ever written to, logged into, or submitted to. CAIS only reads what's already public.

robots.txt honoured

Disallowed paths are never fetched, regardless of what a listing page might otherwise reveal.

Human review required

Every newly discovered source sits pending until a person confirms it belongs on the register.

Nothing auto-activates

A high classifier confidence is evidence for the reviewer, not authority to go live on its own.
